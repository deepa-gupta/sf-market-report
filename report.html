Where the Market Stands

Heading into fall 2026, the San Francisco market has proven remarkably durable. Across 20,184 closed sales through September 15, the 12-month median price is $1.58M — up from $1.50M six months ago — and the YTD median sits at $1.62M. The market has absorbed higher rates, tariff-driven inflation, and broader economic uncertainty without giving back the gains of the past two years. Demand remains structurally supported by tech-sector wealth, and inventory continues to be constrained by rate lock-in.

Fall typically brings a modest slowdown in pace — fewer bidding wars, slightly more days on market — but this is seasonal, not a signal of weakness. The fundamentals driving SF prices have not changed.

What's Driving the Market

The macro picture remains mixed. Tech hiring has stabilized and equity portfolios are near all-time highs, supporting buyer purchasing power at the top end. Inflation has moderated from its early-2026 peak but remains above target, keeping the Federal Reserve on hold. Mortgage rates are still hovering around 6%, and there is no clear catalyst for a significant drop in the near term.

Mortgage rate lock-in continues to suppress listings. Homeowners sitting on 3–4% loans have no financial incentive to sell — and that constrained supply is why prices haven't corrected despite affordability pressure. There is also virtually no distressed inventory; foreclosures remain near historic lows.

If You're Buying a House

Over the past 12 months, 53% of single-family homes sold above asking price, with buyers paying an average of 21.8% over list. The 12-month median for houses is $1.67M, with an average sale price of $2.27M. Average days on market is 34. Noe Valley ($2.00M median), Pacific Heights ($2.90M), and Bernal Heights ($1.60M) are the most active neighborhoods. The fall market offers a window of somewhat lower competition than the spring peak — but well-priced homes still move quickly, and coming in below-list on a desirable property remains a losing strategy in most neighborhoods.

Do not wait for mortgage rates to drop. When rates do ease, pent-up demand will push prices higher and bring more competition back into the market. Buying in a slower season with motivated sellers is often the more advantageous entry point.

If You're Buying a Condo

The condo market continues to offer more room to negotiate than the single-family market. Over the past 12 months, 27% of condos sold above asking (versus 53% for houses), with 36% selling below ask. The 12-month median is $1.15M, and average days on market is 44. South Beach, Inner Mission, and Nob Hill are the highest-volume areas. For buyers who have been priced out of houses, condos represent the most accessible entry point into SF real estate at current pricing.

If You're Selling

The market continues to favor sellers, particularly in the single-family segment. Over the past year, 53% of houses sold above asking at an average premium of 21.8%, and the median DOM of 34 days reflects consistent buyer demand. The fall market typically brings serious, motivated buyers — the window shoppers of spring have moved on, and those still looking in September and October have real intent. Pricing strategy matters: homes priced sharply and cleanly are still attracting multiple offers in the right neighborhoods.

For condos, the dynamics are more nuanced — while 36% of condos sold below asking over the past year, well-presented units in strong locations are still seeing competitive offer situations.

The Moment You're In

The SF market in fall 2026 is not the frenzied spring market, but it is far from soft. Prices are higher year-over-year, inventory is still tight, and the structural forces — tech wealth, rate lock-in, minimal distress — remain intact. For buyers, fall is historically one of the better times to transact: less competition, more motivated sellers, and no less favorable pricing. For sellers in the right neighborhoods, demand remains strong enough to expect a sale at or above market with proper preparation and pricing.
