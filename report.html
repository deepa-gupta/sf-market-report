02 / Editorial analysis

Making sense of the market

Published commentary has its own reporting period and does not change with the data filter.

Where the Market Stands

The numbers don't lie: San Francisco's housing market is running hotter than most observers expected this late in the year. Through November 12, 21,238 verified closed sales are on record. The 12-month median price is $1.63M, and the YTD median is $1.65M — but the story is in the recent quarter. Over the last three months, the median sale price hit $1.72M with 49% of all sales above asking price and median days on market of just 30. For single-family homes, the last quarter is extraordinary: a $2.15M median, 59% over asking, and an average sale price of $3.02M. The fall seasonal slowdown did not arrive.

What's Driving the Market

Mortgage rates have pulled back from their October peak. The 30-year fixed rate is 7.11% as of November 12 (down from 7.40% in early October), and while that is still historically elevated, the marginal easing has brought some buyers back off the sidelines. The Federal Reserve held rates steady at 3.75–4% at its October 27 meeting, though markets are pricing in an 89% probability of another 25-basis-point hike in December. Rates are not about to get easier.

Bay Area tech layoffs continued in the fall — Oracle, Uber, and PayPal cut a combined 1,196 regional jobs in September — but the broader economy is absorbing the impact. The region added 7,800 positions in August across all sectors. The buyers driving the current surge are not vulnerable to these layoffs. At a $3M average sale price for houses, we are talking about a narrow segment of very high-income earners — many of them direct beneficiaries of AI investment — for whom affordability at 7.1% is an inconvenience, not a barrier. Supply lock-in remains the structural constraint it has been all year: homeowners on 3–4% loans have no reason to sell.

If You're Buying a House

The last three months have been the most competitive SF has seen since 2022. 59% of single-family homes sold above asking in Q4, with buyers paying an average of +33.6% over list. The 12-month median is $1.70M, the Q4 median is $2.15M, and median days on market this quarter is 28 days. Noe Valley ($2.25M median), Pacific Heights ($2.995M), and Bernal Heights ($1.655M) are the most active neighborhoods.

If you are waiting for competition to ease, the data says you are waiting for something that is not coming in the near term. Well-priced homes in desirable neighborhoods are receiving multiple offers within days. Bidding strategically means knowing your ceiling before you walk in, waiving contingencies only when you can afford the risk, and writing a clean offer quickly. Hesitation is expensive in this market.

If You're Buying a Condo

Even the condo market, which spent most of 2026 as the more negotiation-friendly option, is tightening. In the last quarter, 45% of condos sold above asking with an average overbid of +18.4% — a sharp shift from the 28%/+11% pace of the full 12-month period. Median DOM this quarter is 30 days. The 12-month condo median is $1.17M; Q4 is tracking to $1.35M.

The window for getting a condo below asking has narrowed meaningfully. That said, the 12-month picture — 35% under asking, 42-day median DOM — reflects real periods of softness earlier in the year. You still have more room to negotiate on a condo than on a house, particularly for units with longer-term softness like high-HOA buildings or older stock. South Beach, Inner Mission, and Nob Hill have the highest volume.

If You're Selling

This is as good as it has been in years. Single-family sellers who price well are routinely seeing offers 20–30% above list in the best neighborhoods. The fall market has brought serious, qualified buyers — not casual browsers — and the lack of competing listings is working in your favor. If you have been waiting for the right moment to list, that moment is now or early spring before inventory seasonally increases.

Condo sellers should temper expectations relative to houses, but the recent data is encouraging. Condos are performing significantly better in Q4 than they did mid-year. Present the unit well, price at current comps (not 2024 comps), and you have a realistic path to multiple offers in the right building and neighborhood.

The Moment You're In

November 2026 is showing one of the strongest fall markets SF has had in years, driven by a narrow but powerful buyer pool, structurally constrained inventory, and mortgage rates that — while elevated — have eased from their October peak. A December Fed hike would push rates back up and could cool things heading into 2027. For buyers with the financial position to move, acting before year-end avoids that risk. For sellers, the Q4 window is open and competitive — use it.
